How to play

Each round shows a famous painting as a blurry mosaic of colored tiles. Name it before it comes into focus.

The basics

  1. A game is five paintings.
  2. Each painting starts as a coarse mosaic of just a few tiles. Type the painting’s title and press Guess.
  3. A wrong guess sharpens the mosaic one step. You can also press Sharpen it to see more without guessing.
  4. There are six views, from a handful of tiles to the full painting. The sooner you name it, the more points you score.

You don’t need the exact title. Common English names, original-language titles and small typos are accepted, and suggestions appear as you type.

Help, at a price

  • Hint shows the artist’s nationality and the century the painting was made, and costs 100 points.
  • Show 4 choices turns the round into multiple choice, each with the artist’s name, and halves the points.
  • Hard mode shows the mosaic in black and white until the fourth view.

Today’s five

Everyone gets the same five paintings each day, drawn from the whole collection. A new puzzle starts at midnight in your time zone. You can play Today’s five once; after that, your results stay on screen until the next puzzle.

Finish Today’s five on consecutive days to build a streak. Your games played, current streak and best streak are kept in your own browser, so they don’t follow you to another device.

Categories

Besides Today’s five, you can play as many games as you like from a single category:

  • Famous 50: the paintings almost everyone knows, from the Mona Lisa to The Starry Night. A good place to start.
  • Renaissance: Europe in the 1400s and 1500s, from Botticelli and Leonardo to El Greco.
  • Baroque: the drama and deep shadows of the 1600s and 1700s: Rembrandt, Velázquez, Vermeer and Tiepolo.
  • Dutch Golden Age: the Netherlands in the 1600s: Rembrandt, Vermeer, Frans Hals, and the everyday scenes, landscapes and still lifes the Dutch made famous.
  • Romanticism: emotion, drama and wild nature from the late 1700s to the mid-1800s: Turner, Constable, Friedrich, Delacroix and Goya.
  • Impressionism: Monet, Renoir, Degas and friends in the 1870s and 1880s, painting light and modern life.
  • Post-Impressionism: Van Gogh, Cézanne, Gauguin, Seurat and Toulouse-Lautrec, who took color and form in bold new directions.
  • Everything: the whole collection mixed together.

After each painting, a museum-style label tells you who made it, when, where it hangs and one thing worth knowing about it.
