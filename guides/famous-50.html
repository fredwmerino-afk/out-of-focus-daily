Famous 50

1400s–1900s · The paintings almost everyone knows, from the Mona Lisa to The Starry Night.

Some paintings have escaped the museum and become part of everyday life. They turn up on posters, coffee mugs, cartoons and advertisements, and many people can picture them without ever having seen the original. This collection gathers fifty of those icons, spanning more than five hundred years of European, American and Japanese art.

What makes a painting famous? Sometimes it is sheer mastery, as with Leonardo’s Mona Lisa or Vermeer’s Girl with a Pearl Earring. Sometimes it is a story: the Mona Lisa became a household name only after it was stolen from the Louvre in 1911. Some works were shocking in their day, like Manet’s Olympia, and others captured a moment in history, like Goya’s Third of May 1808 or Delacroix’s Liberty Leading the People.

If you are new to Out of Focus, this is the best place to start. The shapes and colors of these pictures are so familiar that many players can name them from just a handful of mosaic tiles.

What to look for

  • A strong, simple composition you can recognize from far away
  • Bold color contrasts, like the yellow and blue of Van Gogh’s Café Terrace at Night
  • A single striking figure or shape: the screaming face, the great wave, the woman in the pearl earring
