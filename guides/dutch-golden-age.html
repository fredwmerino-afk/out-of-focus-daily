Dutch Golden Age

c. 1600–1700 · Rembrandt, Vermeer, Frans Hals, and a small country that painted the world around it.

In the 1600s the newly independent Dutch Republic became one of the richest places on earth, thanks to trade and shipping. Its prosperous merchants wanted art for their homes, and thousands of painters set to work to supply them. There were so many that artists specialized: some painted only seascapes, others only flowers, church interiors or winter skating scenes.

Because the Protestant Dutch church did not commission religious images, painters turned to everyday life. Johannes Vermeer captured quiet rooms filled with soft daylight. Frans Hals painted portraits so lively they seem to laugh. Rembrandt van Rijn, the greatest of them all, painted group portraits, Bible stories and dozens of searching self-portraits.

Dutch still lifes, with their gleaming silver, half-peeled lemons and bouquets of flowers that bloom in different seasons, often carried a hidden message: life and its pleasures are brief.

What to look for

  • Light from a window falling across a quiet interior
  • Low horizons under huge, cloudy skies
  • Tables of food, glass and silver painted in astonishing detail

Key artists

  • Hendrick Avercamp
  • Frans Hals
  • Rembrandt van Rijn
  • Johannes Vermeer
