Post-Impressionism

c. 1885–1905 · Van Gogh, Cézanne, Gauguin and Seurat take color and form in bold new directions.

Post-Impressionism is not one style but a name for the very different artists who built on Impressionism in the 1880s and 1890s and pushed past it. They kept its bright colors but wanted something more solid, more personal or more symbolic than a fleeting impression of light.

Georges Seurat built his pictures from thousands of tiny dots of pure color, a technique called pointillism. Paul Cézanne broke objects into geometric planes and patches of color, and later painters called him the father of modern art. Vincent van Gogh used swirling brushstrokes and intense color to express emotion, and Paul Gauguin traveled to Tahiti in search of a simpler life, painting it in flat areas of brilliant color. Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec captured the nightlife of Montmartre in paintings and posters.

Their experiments opened the door to the art of the twentieth century, from Fauvism and Cubism to Expressionism.

What to look for

  • Thick, swirling or patterned brushstrokes
  • Unnatural, expressive color: yellow skies, red trees, purple shadows
  • Simplified, flattened shapes and strong outlines

Key artists

  • Paul Cézanne
  • Georges Seurat
  • Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec
  • Vincent van Gogh
  • Paul Gauguin
  • Edvard Munch
