Baroque

c. 1600–1750 · Drama, deep shadows and dazzling light: the art of the 1600s and early 1700s.

Baroque art was made to move people. After the calm balance of the Renaissance, painters of the 1600s turned to strong contrasts of light and dark, swirling movement and intense emotion. The Italian painter Caravaggio led the way, lighting his figures as if by a single spotlight against black backgrounds, a technique called chiaroscuro that spread across Europe.

In Catholic Italy and Spain, the Baroque was often the art of the Church, meant to inspire devotion: think of the ecstatic saints of Guido Reni or the austere still lifes and crucifixions of Zurbarán. At the Spanish court, Diego Velázquez painted kings and servants with the same honesty. In the Protestant Dutch Republic, painters turned instead to portraits, landscapes and scenes of everyday life.

By the early 1700s the style had lightened into the Rococo, with the pastel colors and playful scenes of painters like Boucher and Tiepolo, which are also included here.

What to look for

  • Dramatic light falling on figures out of darkness
  • Diagonal compositions full of movement
  • Rich still lifes of food, flowers and game

Key artists

  • Hendrick Avercamp
  • Diego Velázquez
  • Frans Hals
  • Rembrandt van Rijn
  • Johannes Vermeer
  • Giovanni Battista Tiepolo
